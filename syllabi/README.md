# Syllabi

One syllabus per term, in chronological order. **A syllabus is never edited once its term has
started** — it is the record of what students were actually told, and correcting it after the fact
destroys exactly the thing it is kept for. Start the next term by copying the previous file, not by
editing it.

## Why these live here rather than on the course site

The public course site carries **materials** and nothing specific to one university, term or
section. A syllabus is the opposite of that: room, meeting time, grading scale, academic-integrity
and accessibility policies, the term calendar. So it is **not published** to the site — Canvas
carries the copy every enrolled student reads, and this directory is the archive.

What a public visitor needs from a syllabus — what the course teaches, what it assumes you know,
which books it uses — is on the site as [`ECON8310_About.md`](../ECON8310_About.md).

## The fall/spring split

The course runs **in person in fall and fully online in spring**, and this is where that difference
belongs: two honest documents, rather than one straining to describe both. Everything that differs
between the two is an enrollment fact and therefore lives in a syllabus, not in the materials —
the room, the meeting time, deadlines anchored to "before class begins", the in-person meeting,
office-hours modality, and the presentation format.

The **week numbers must stay aligned with the course site**, which lists 16 undated teaching weeks.
That works because the break week is *named, not numbered* — "Thanksgiving Week" in fall, "Spring
Break" in spring — so the numbering runs continuously across it and Canvas's Week 14 is the site's
Week 14 in both terms.

## When you write the next one

Copy the most recent file, then check every item below, because each one is known to differ by term
or by modality:

- [ ] Term, dates, and the whole weekly schedule
- [ ] Meeting time and room — or the online equivalent
- [ ] The break week's name and position
- [ ] Office hours, and whether Zoom is the primary channel or a fallback
- [ ] The required one-on-one meeting: in person or over Zoom
- [ ] Homework deadline anchor — "before class begins" has no meaning in an async section
- [ ] Presentation format: in person, live online, or recorded
- [ ] Any one-off events (the Fall 2026 industry panel, the Week 5 asynchronous session)
- [ ] Add a row to the table above

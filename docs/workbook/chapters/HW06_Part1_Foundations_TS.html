23  Homework 6: Bayesian Foundations, Time Series, and Hierarchical Models

Due Week 13. Covers Lecture 10 (foundations) and Lecture 11 Part 1 (structural time series).

Your numbers will not match mine, and that is fine

Every result in this course depends on a random seed, and often also on your library versions, your hardware, and how many CPU threads your machine uses. Two people running identical, correct code will get different numbers — sometimes noticeably different.

What is graded is that you used the right model and the right procedure, and that your interpretation follows from the numbers you actually obtained. Quote your own output.

What is not graded is matching any particular value. Do not tune your way toward a number you saw on a slide or heard from a classmate — that is the one way to turn this into a wrong answer.

One caveat worth knowing. The broad ordering of models usually survives this variation. If yours comes out backwards from what the lecture found, say so in a sentence and offer a reason: either you have found something real about your run, or there is a bug worth hunting. Both are worth more marks than quietly reporting a number you do not believe.

23.1 Overview

Two things separate a Bayesian forecast from everything else in this course: you state your assumptions as priors and can check them before fitting, and the answer is a distribution rather than a number.

Part 1 does both on a single series. You will check a prior before trusting it, fit a Beta-Binomial by hand, then build a structural time-series model with a random-walk level and Fourier seasonality — and judge its forecast on two criteria, accuracy and honesty.

Learning objectives. By the end you will be able to:

  • Run and read a prior predictive check, and say what it rules out
  • Fit and diagnose a PyMC model, reporting \(\hat{R}\), ESS and divergences
  • Decompose a series into level and seasonality with priors on each
  • Judge a predictive interval on both calibration and usefulness

Report diagnostics with every model

Lecture 10 gave you three checks, and this assignment expects all three for every model you fit: \(\hat{R} < 1.01\), ESS \(> 400\), and zero divergences. az.summary(idata, ci_kind="hdi", ci_prob=0.94) gives the first two; pm.sample reports divergences as it runs.

A model that fails these has not converged, and its posterior means nothing — report the failure rather than the numbers.

23.4.1 Q1 — Prior Predictive Check (Suggested prompts: 3)

Estimate the probability that any given week is a high-SNAP week. Model the count out of CA_1 FOODS’s 277 weeks as Beta-Binomial:

  • Prior: theta ~ Beta(2, 2) — weakly informative, centered at 0.5
  • Likelihood: obs ~ Binomial(n=277, p=theta), observed = the actual count

Before fitting anything, run pm.sample_prior_predictive(500) and plot what the prior alone implies about the number of high-SNAP weeks.
